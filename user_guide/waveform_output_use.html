Waveform output NIDAQmx_Waveform

The actuator NIDAQmx_Waveform (DAQ_Move_NIDAQmx_Waveform) plays waveforms, arrays of samples, on several analog outputs of one NI device at once. The outputs are driven by one task, so they share a sample clock and start together. For one output set a level at a time, see NIDAQmx; for voltage ramps timed by a counter, see NIDAQmx_RampOutput.

The position of the actuator is a DataActuator holding one array of samples (in volts) for each chosen output, all of the same length. The arrays are given in the order of the chosen outputs. The plugin follows the contract of pymodaq_plugins_utils.hardware.waveform_output: the applications that design waveforms, such as the extension of pymodaq_plugins_waveform, read what this device can do and check what they send before they send it.

How to use it

  1. Choose the Outputs (the analog outputs of one device, ticked in the list) and their range (Min, Max). Outputs of several devices cannot be in one task: the plugin refuses them. Change them while the actuator runs and the outputs go to 0 V before the new ones are driven.

  2. In Waveform output choose the Sample rate, the Mode and the number of Repetitions:

    • finite: the samples are generated once;

    • repeat: the samples are generated Repetitions times;

    • continuous: the samples are generated again and again until the actuator is stopped. The driver repeats the samples that were written, so the waveform should hold a whole number of periods.

  3. Optionally give an external Sample clock (a terminal such as /Dev1/PFI1) and a Start trigger (a PFI terminal and its edge): the generation then waits for the edge. Without trigger it starts at once.

  4. Move the actuator to the samples.

The move is done when the generation has started. The end of a finite or repeated generation is announced by the status command generation_done (0 if all went well, else the code of the driver error). The outputs are at 0 V when the actuator starts, after stop and when it closes. When a finite waveform ends, the outputs keep its last sample until the next waveform or the stop.

Limits

The settings group Waveform capabilities (read only) shows what the plugin tells to the applications: the number of outputs, the range of sample rates (ao_min_rate and ao_max_rate of the device), the voltage range (the intersection of Min, Max and the ranges of the device), the modes, the PFI terminals if the device accepts a start trigger, and Max samples.

  • The device may be slower than its ao_max_rate when several outputs are used (some modules share their converter between outputs): the driver then refuses the rate and its message is shown.

  • Max samples is a limit of the plugin (samples per output, to protect the memory), not a figure of the device. Set it as you need; a longer waveform is refused with a message.

  • A new waveform replaces the one being played with a break: the outputs go through 0 V in between (live_update is False). To change a waveform without a break, a device that can update its buffer on the fly is needed.

  • The samples are written to the buffer of the driver before the generation starts: there is no streaming of waveforms longer than the buffer.
